The Correlator Ledger

Walsh–Hadamard spectra of Gibbs priors over map descriptors: pj ∝ e−Ej, Ej = γCj + λFj + μWj (circuit complexity; footprint F = n·I + S, combining image size and support; weight bias). Slide the fields and watch where the prior stores its knowledge: every correlator p̂(c) = Σj pj(−1)popcount(j∧c), one per test mask c.

Three exact laws (temperature-independent)

These hold for the untouched prior — at every γ, which is why some readouts ignore that slider. The λ (footprint) field couples to a relabeling-invariant descriptor, so laws 1–3 survive it too. Two things break laws 1–2: evidence, and the μ (weight) field, which couples to W — odd under output negation — and acts as a magnetic field: slide it and watch the odd shells and the on-site tile switch on before any question is asked. Law 3 (distance-only pair correlators) survives all three fields, since every descriptor is input-relabeling invariant.

  1. Every odd shell vanishes identically. Circuit complexity is invariant under negating the output(s), which acts on truth tables as the translation j ↦ j ⊕ 1…1; translations multiply p̂(c) by (−1)popcount(c), so invariance forces every odd-popcount correlator to zero. For 3→2, each output bit can be negated separately — the mask must have even popcount in each bit-plane.
  2. M stays exactly uniform at every temperature. Law 1 kills all on-site correlators (single sites always; the within-answer pair for 3→2 has one site per plane), and the on-site ledger is M. The Gibbs prior has zero predictive capital before the first answer: 100% of its knowledge is cross-question correlation — the stored bits m·2ⁿ − H(p) are all convertible, none predictive. A complexity prior is pure “instinct for patterns,” no instinct for answers.
  3. Pair correlators depend only on the Hamming distance between the two inputs (the input-relabeling group is transitive on pairs at fixed distance — within-class spread is 0 to machine precision). Nearby inputs correlate positively, antipodal inputs anticorrelate, and as γ→∞ the curve approaches the closed form 1 − 2d/(n+1) (from the gate-free tier: 2 constants + 2n literals). Circuit structure appears in the ledger as geometry: simple circuits are smooth functions on the input hypercube.

…and what asking a question does to them

  1. Each answer collapses the asked block and cashes cross-correlators into predictions. In the ledger, learning ψ(qk)=a* averages every correlator with its 2m partners (its toggles inside the asked block) — the asked block saturates to ±1, and every unasked column of M moves by exactly the covariance that linked it to the asked block. Watch the on-site energy tile leave 0 on the first click: that is law 2 breaking, and it is the only thing in this system that can break it.
  2. The greedy button is the lemma ΔH(q) = HB(q). The expected drop in belief entropy from asking q equals the current answer entropy of column q, so the best question is simply the most uncertain column of M — the same number shown in every unasked button’s tooltip. How much the other columns sharpen depends on the prior’s cross-question ledger; with this prior, that is where all the knowledge lives.
  3. Support and ledger deflate together, by a factor 2m per question. Survivors number 2m(2ⁿ−k) after k answers — independent of which questions were asked or what came back — and correlators touching asked blocks become signed copies of untouched ones, so the independent ledger shrinks at the same geometric rate. Once ≤16 hypotheses survive, the panel enumerates them: for (2,1) that is the whole game; for the 65536-map spaces it is the endgame, where the posterior has usually singled out the truth well before the last questions pin it.
